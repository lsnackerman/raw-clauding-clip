# /clip — learnings

The reasoning behind the steps in SKILL.md, and the cases each one came from. When a cut lands wrong in a way this file doesn't cover, add the case here.

## Origin — 2026-10-17

It started as two shout-outs to post: a guest on the BrewFOs podcast and a guest on *Beyond With Sean*, each saying what the raw-clauding way of working had done for them. Four clips later the recipe was clear enough to package. It was built on the second show rather than the first, so the parts that vary between shows (layout, resolution, audio tracks) were known before anything was hard-coded.

## What each lesson cost

- **Ask for the format.** yt-dlp's default gave a ~290 kbps AV1 stream on BrewFOs: soft, and AV1 is a poor upload format. Asking for H.264 doubled the bitrate.
- **Original-language audio.** *Beyond With Sean* carries YouTube auto-dubs in ~20 languages. A hand-picked format number could have been one of them; the format selector now asks for the track marked `original`.
- **Captions downloaded when the standard pull didn't.** Plain `--sub-langs en` hit HTTP 429 three times; `en-orig` came through on the first try.
- **xfade needs a declared frame rate.** It rejected the segments until each got `setpts → fps → settb`, in that order (`fps` before `setpts` failed the same way).
- **The ending.** The first spliced clip "ends a smidge short": a 0.25s fade-out started before the last word finished. Fix: stop the audio ~40ms after the word, hold the last frame ~0.7s in silence, fade the picture. **Then it still sounded short, so two longer variants went over, and hearing them showed the version already made was right.** The lesson isn't the milliseconds: when the cut is a judgment call, send variants, don't iterate blind.
- **A dip isn't always a boundary.** On that clip, the loudest-looking gap after the last word was partway into a drawn-out word, and the captions put "you know" in the stretch after it. Levels say where it's quiet, not what the words are.
- **Layouts vary per show.** BrewFOs is two big side-by-side panels; *Beyond* is a slide with 230px webcams. That's why layout is spec input and `layouts.md` exists.
- **Word-timing parser.** Auto-caption cues repeat the previous line above the new one, and a short line can arrive with no per-word tags (a lone "coding."). Both broke the first parser.
- **Frame 1 is the thumbnail.** A finished clip started black, which is what a feed shows until someone hits play. Two causes stacked: a 0.15s video fade-in, and, on vertical layouts, the black canvas starting one frame before the cropped pieces arrived (the pieces still carried the source's timestamps). Fixed both (no picture fade-in; zero timestamps before compositing) and started the first caption at 0.0 so the preview carries the quote. **Every check before that looked at frames from the middle**, which is why none caught it; the skill now says to read frame 0.
- **The cold test, and the one it caught that mattered.** Before sharing, a fresh agent got only this folder and made a clip from an episode nothing was tuned on. The setup and the BrewFOs preset held. It found four real bugs: a dangling ffmpeg link would crash every command after `uv cache clean`; `out` resolved from the current folder, not the spec's; captions didn't fade with the picture; and **the splice dissolve overlapped the audio too**, so any syllable within 0.3s of a cut got swallowed (the "ends a smidge short" problem, arriving through the join). Its cut points had under 0.1s of quiet, which no audio crossfade survives. **Fix: the picture dissolves, the sound cuts** (the next segment's video starts early as pre-roll), now the default. It also found the missing first step: with nobody to say roughly where the moment is, a 14,000-word list is unreadable, hence `transcript`. And the "0.1s early" caption offset didn't hold on that episode (some words on time, some late), so it's a hint, not a rule. *The join check's first version also flagged an approved splice; narrowed to the half-window next to the join, it passes that and still catches the bad one. A check that fires on approved work gets ignored.*
- **The iPhone strip, and how the investigation went wrong before it went right.** An iPhone showed a dashed white strip down the right side of vertical clips made from Zoom recordings; computers showed nothing. **Cause: Zoom stamps its recordings with the old standard-definition color matrix (`bt470bg`), it rode straight through to the clips, and iPhones draw that strip on vertical video tagged that way.** `render` now always converts to and tags standard HD color (`bt709`). Clips from YouTube were never affected. Check a source with `ffmpeg -i FILE`: `yuv420p(tv, bt470bg…` is the tag that hit it.
  - **The detour cost seven rounds of tests on the phone, and it was self-inflicted.** The first theory (1080 isn't a multiple of 16, so the iPhone ignores a crop flag) went into the tool as a fix, 1152×2048, before it was confirmed. **That fix caused the same strip**, so every test after it came back positive whatever it changed: color, frame rate, audio rate, layout, length, captions. A one-change-at-a-time bisect with a known-clean control (an older clip, rebuilt with the old pipeline, checked frame-for-frame identical to the clean file) found the resize in one round. Rerun at 1080, the color fix alone cleared it.
  - **The lessons:** don't ship a fix for an unconfirmed cause into the thing you're testing with; change one variable per test; keep a control that should come back clean. And the device's report is the only measurement: the strip never appeared in the decoded pixels, color channels, encoder settings or container, so "it's not in the file" was true and useless.
  - **And the vertical's tags themselves.** After the bt709 fix, the 9:16 still read primaries and transfer "unknown": the black canvas carries no tags, and the encode flags didn't override the frames'. `setparams` after the `scale` fixed it, with the picture unchanged.
- **A hold that never happened.** To start the speaker's sound on her first word while a meeting tile still showed someone else, the first segment held her frame for 0.6s (`video_start`). `tpad` pads the start only when it knows the frame rate, and Zoom records variable-rate, so it silently added nothing: the picture started 0.6s early, ran ahead of her voice for the whole segment, and the video stream ended 4.6s before the file did. **Every check passed**: frame 0 looked right, the held frames looked still (they were just still footage), durations printed fine. The human caught it by watching the slowed-down version ("the audio sounds great but the video is at the regular speed"). Fix: `fps` before `tpad`; and `render` now checks that the picture runs the full length of the clip, which is the measurement that would have caught it.
- **Room light is part of the look.** One section of a spliced clip looked warmer and better than the other. Both recordings carried the same color tags, so nothing in `render` caused it: a lamp was set differently. The fix is at the recording, and the preference goes into choosing between equally good tellings. A frame from each recording, side by side, is the quick way to see it.
- **Brand spelling in captions.** Spell a brand or coined term the way its owner writes it (a hyphen, a capital). The spoken words are the same; the spelling is the caption-maker's to get right.
- **Homebrew's ffmpeg can't burn captions.** It's built without libass, so there's no `subtitles` filter; every command but `render` worked. `ffmpeg_path(captions=True)` now checks and falls through to uv's static build.
- **A stale yt-dlp fails only on the video.** An old Homebrew yt-dlp read the title fine, then got `403 Forbidden` on the slice; `brew upgrade yt-dlp` fixed it. A working `vtt` says nothing about `fetch`.
- **The first re-make on a second machine matched.** The same recipe on another Mac: same frame counts, picture SSIM 0.998, sound bit-identical. A recipe is a reliable record of a clip.
- **Build captions from the word list by script, not by hand**, and match anchors to the word list in order with two- or three-word keys. Snapping each caption to the nearest instance of its first word put 3 of 72 on the wrong spot ("and I" is inside "my wife and I"); a caption under 0.6s is the tell. Drop an interjection that would flash under half a second.
- **Convert source times to slice times by script, never in your head.** Three hand conversions landed 20 seconds off, and the levels at the wrong spots looked plausible. A transcriber check on a cut point also checks that you're looking at the right place.
- **A show graphic across the panel seam needs its own segment and layout** (a map over the seam of the stacked vertical). Hence per-segment `"formats"` and `"crossfade": 0`. ⚠️ The crop is `[w, h, x, y]`; typing x,y first came out small and clipped, and only reading frames caught it.
- ⚠️ **An approved clip shipped with b-roll sliced by the vertical's crop, and every check had sampled around it.** A few frames can't prove a vertical; scene detection over the whole range can. Hence `cuts`, run on the whole frame, the graphics box, and the text-caption band. **A contact sheet across the whole moment** tells you what kinds of shot exist before you pick a detector.
- **A dissolve reads footage from before the segment's start** (the pre-roll). A piece starting just after a shot change showed the previous shot ghosting through the dissolve. Check that `start − crossfade` is still the same shot.
- **The clip's first word gets the deepest silence available.** A −45 dB dip sat inside "basically"; Whisper transcribed the test cut cleanly, and the human heard the clipped word at once. **A transcriber is not a check that a cut keeps its first word whole:** it fills fragments in from context. To settle a disputed word, transcribe a 2–3s snippet, not the passage (on a long passage it fills in grammar); split a disputed spot into its parts and transcribe each.
- **When the only gap is short, measure it at 5 ms.** `levels` at 20 ms showed one −54 dB window; the raw samples showed 60 ms of room tone, enough for a clean start.
- **End where the speaker ends, not where a sentence ends** (twice, once with a full second of silence after the "last" word). The test is whether the next sentence carries the same thought, not the length of the gap. **A soft last word needs room before the fade:** run `end` to just before the next voice and raise `hold` (1.2s there).
- **A conjunction left at a splice reads wrong** (*"Baghdad, Iraq, and"* → dissolve → *"So I'm outside"*). End a segment on a sentence's end.
- **Asked for a change, change only that**, and offer two versions when a note could be met two ways.
